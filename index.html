Flying Whales — How It All Began

Our Story: Flying Whales

Back in 2011, a young girl started her career in Sri Lanka. With her team, she worked on building roads and bridges.

Once, during a sudden, massive flood, she came dangerously close to losing her life. But thankfully, with a help from the spirits of nature, she and her team made it through alive.

When she returned home, she wanted to honor that unforgettable experience. So, she started a small company. She called it Flying Whales, because if whales could take to the skies, they would be the happiest creatures alive. That is the origin of our brand.

We were officially established in 2016. Riding the wave of China's cross-border e-commerce boom, we have delivered our goods to customers worldwide .

She loves seeing her customers wear the clothes she sells. Having seen mothers and children from different cultures, she realized how beautifully similar we all are.

Flying Whales is a Chinese trading company located in Guangzhou. To learn more about our journey, please check out our local website www.flyingwhales.cn.

First Impressions

A city that refuses to choose between old and new.

Colonial arcades and bamboo scaffolding, jade-tea houses and 600-metre towers, all washed by the same tide. Guangzhou doesn't perform its history — it serves it with hot water and a towel.

01 / Water

River, Islands & Bridges

The Pearl River is the city's spine. Morning light on Shamian's embankment, the walk from Ersha Island to Haixinsha at golden hour — Guangzhou is best understood at water level.

02 / Ritual

Dim Sum as a Slow Art

Yum cha is not a meal, it's a morning well spent. Tiny steamers, endlessly refilled tea, aunties wheeling carts — the city's real clock ticks in bamboo.

Lanes with a Second Life

In Yongqing Fang, Lingnan tile roofs share a wall with indie bookshops and cocktail bars. The past isn't preserved under glass here — it's still renting the flat upstairs.

A Short Menu

Eat the way Guangzhou counts the hours.

If you take only one thing from this page: let a local decide your dinner. Until then, start here.

¥28

Har Gow & Siu Mai

Translucent shrimp dumplings and pork-shrimp open tops. The silent test of any tea house — if the skin holds, you're in good hands.

¥22

Char Siu Bao

Steamed, never baked, at a proper yum-cha table. Sweet, smoky, slightly absurdly soft. Order two. No one orders one.

¥25

Double-Skin Milk

A Cantonese dessert of ridiculous simplicity: milk, egg, sugar, and a skin that forms twice. Served cold or warm, never rushed.

¥32

Old-School Noodle Soup

Bamboo-pressed noodles with a broth that's been simmering since breakfast. Add chives, add liver if you dare, eat standing up.
